How to Calculate Mortgage Payments in Canada | Go LANA

Understand how Canadian mortgage payments work and use a free mortgage calculator to estimate your monthly costs.

Calculating mortgage payments in Canada differs from other countries in several important ways. Canadian mortgages typically have a maximum amortization period of 25 years for insured mortgages and 30 years for uninsured ones. Interest is compounded semi-annually, not monthly, which affects the effective rate.

The mortgage payment formula accounts for the principal amount, the interest rate, and the amortization period. In Canada, the effective monthly rate is calculated from the annual rate compounded semi-annually. This means the monthly rate is slightly lower than the annual rate divided by 12.

To calculate your mortgage payment, you need three numbers: the purchase price minus your down payment (the principal), the annual interest rate, and the amortization period in years. A mortgage calculator handles the math automatically, but understanding the inputs helps you make better decisions.

The down payment in Canada follows a tiered system. For homes under 500,000 CAD, the minimum down payment is 5 percent. For homes between 500,000 and 999,999 CAD, it is 5 percent on the first 500,000 and 10 percent on the remainder. For homes above 1 million CAD, the minimum is 20 percent.

If your down payment is less than 20 percent, you must get mortgage default insurance through CMHC, Sagen, or Canada Guaranty. This insurance premium is added to your mortgage amount and affects your monthly payment. The premium ranges from 2.8 to 4 percent of the mortgage amount depending on the loan-to-value ratio.

Property taxes, home insurance, and potentially condo fees should be added to your monthly housing cost. These are not part of the mortgage payment itself but are essential for budgeting. Some calculators include these as optional fields.

Interest rates in Canada can be fixed or variable. Fixed rates stay the same for the term, typically 1 to 5 years. Variable rates fluctuate with the lender prime rate. Your choice affects how much interest you pay over the term and how your payment may change at renewal.

Use a mortgage calculator to compare scenarios. Try different home prices, down payments, interest rates, and amortization periods. Even a small change in the rate or amortization can significantly affect the total interest paid over the life of the mortgage. The calculator shows monthly payment, total interest, and total cost.
